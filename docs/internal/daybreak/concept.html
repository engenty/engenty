Concept · engenty-pro · start page

Daybreak.
The start page as chief of staff.

You walk in at 7:15. The screen isn't a grid of empty widgets waiting for you to click — it's a briefing that was composed for you at 6:30: what needs your decision, what changed overnight, what's already prepared, and a conversation ready to continue. Think the perfect assistant from The Devil Wears Prada; think a chief of staff who worked the early shift.

replaces · legacy dashboard (retired) + per-module briefing pages substrate · generative-ui (json-render) · heartbeats · connections · pdf-service surfaces · web · mobile · voice · email/PDF · Slack/Teams

A dashboard shows you the state of the system. A briefing tells you what to do about it — and has already done everything that didn't need you.

01 · Stance

Five principles

P1

Composed, not configured

Nobody arranges widgets. An agent composes the page each morning from what actually matters today — the layout is an output, not a setting. Generated UI via the json-render catalog, not hand-built dashboards.

P2

Decisions first

The scarcest resource is the user's judgment. Everything that needs a human verdict is at the top, pre-worked: context gathered, draft written, one tap to approve, edit, or defer.

P3

Work happened overnight

The briefing is the visible tip of autonomous work: inbox triaged, payments matched, drafts written, tenders scanned online. The page reports outcomes, and only escalates what policy says it must.

P4

It's a conversation

Every element is an entry point into chat. Tap a metric, ask "why", say "push everything to tomorrow". The briefing is the first message of the day's session, not a separate app.

P5

One briefing, many bodies

The same BriefingSpec renders interactive on web and mobile, spoken over voice, as a PNG/PDF in email or WhatsApp, as action cards in Slack. Reduced channels still act — "reply APPROVE 2041".

P6

Grounded & fresh

Not just internal state: the composition step checks the outside world — tender portals, news about counterparties, price lists — and folds what's relevant into the brief with sources.

The agent doesn't fill a fixed template — it selects, ranks, and drops sections. A quiet day is three lines and "nothing needs you"; a loud day leads with five decisions. Composition is cheap to re-run, so "refresh my briefing" mid-day is the same flow with a smaller window.

B — Decision · approve, edit, defer

Any surfaceDecision cardweb · phone · Slack button · voice "send it"
QueueDecision recordone queue, resolved once, idempotent everywhere
ApprovalsSuspend / resumenative tool approvals; grant written back
ExecuteConnector actionsend Gmail · post Slack · under write policy
Close loopCard → log linebadge drops · other surfaces update · "Defer" reschedules a trigger

"Defer to 12:00" is not a snooze on a notification — it creates a one-shot trigger that re-runs the decision's context first. If Kranz pays at 10:40, the deferred reminder dissolves itself and the noon briefing says so.

C — Drill-in · from glance to conversation

WidgetTap "Overdue €21.3k"catalog sendMessage action fires
CopilotScoped sessionwidget context + module tools attached
AnswerGenerated detail viewtable of the 3 invoices, aging, last contact
Act"Chase all three"drafts queue as new decision cards

The briefing page and the chat are one continuum: the page is the agent's opening message, rendered large. Drilling in never navigates away — it deepens the same session, and new decisions flow back into the same queue.

08 · Path

Four phases, each independently shippable

PHASE 1

One page

New modules/briefing plugin. The composer agent pulls existing module briefing services + inbox digest on a morning heartbeat and emits a BriefingSpec rendered by generative-ui. Replaces the per-module briefing pages as the start route. No decisions yet — awareness only, but composed, ranked, and conversational.

ships: briefing-provider contract · hb_briefing heartbeat · BriefingSpec + 3 new catalog widgets · default route

PHASE 2

Decisions

The decision queue: connections ask-policy items, approval-gated module actions, and agent-drafted work become cards with approve/edit/defer. Defer creates one-shot triggers that re-check context before resurfacing.

ships: decision store · approvals integration · defer-as-trigger · "handled without you" ledger

PHASE 3

Channels

BriefingSpec → PDF/PNG via pdf-service; delivery through Gmail/Slack connectors under their write policies. Reply-to-act lands in the existing inbox pipeline. Slack/Teams action cards resolve against the same decision queue.

ships: briefing pdf template · outbound delivery · reply parsing · slack card actions

PHASE 4

Voice & rhythm

Narrated briefing over the realtime voice channel with decision tools; evening wrap heartbeat; on-demand micro-briefings ("brief me on X before the meeting"). The page stops being a morning event and becomes the day's cadence.

ships: narration render target · voice decision tools · evening heartbeat · scoped micro-briefs

09 · Honest edges

Open questions worth deciding early

Trust ramp

The "handled without you" ledger is the trust instrument — every autonomous act visible and reversible. But the default autonomy level per tenant (read-only vs. draft-everything) should be an explicit onboarding choice, not a config buried in connections.

Tenant vs. user briefing

The inbox thread is tenant-scoped; the briefing is personal. Composition needs a per-user view over tenant data — roles decide whose decisions land on whose page. A two-person shop wants overlap; a ten-person one wants routing.

Cost of composition

A full compose touches every module, three connectors, and the web — per user, per morning, in the tenant's AI-plane sandbox. Needs a budget: incremental since-last-brief windows, cached module summaries, and a cheap "quiet day" early exit.

When the agent is wrong

A briefing that misranks once is forgiven; one that buries a critical item loses the user. Every dropped-or-demoted item needs a "everything else" overflow view, and "why did you rank this here?" must always have an answer.
